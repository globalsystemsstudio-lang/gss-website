Structure a sales page for: **$ARGUMENTS**

Write it in reading order, because that's the order belief gets built:

1. **Headline** — the outcome, in the reader's language. Not the product name. Three versions, then say which you'd ship.
2. **Subhead** — who it's for and the mechanism, one sentence.
3. **The problem** — described so precisely they feel recognized. This section earns the right to everything below it. Get it wrong and nothing else is read.
4. **Why the usual approach fails** — what they've already tried, and why it didn't hold. Names the frustration before offering the fix.
5. **The offer** — what it is and how it works. Plain, specific, no mystique.
6. **What's included** — concrete deliverables. Access, duration, format, quantity.
7. **Proof** — testimonials, results, credentials, track record. Mark clearly as `[proof needed: X]` where you don't have real material. **Never fabricate a testimonial, a result, or a number.**
8. **Objections** — the four that actually stop the sale, answered in the reader's order of arrival: *Will it work for me? Can I afford it? Do I have time? What if it doesn't work?*
9. **Pricing** — presented with the value framing next to it. Use only prices I've given you or that exist in the repo's own standards file; if you don't have one, write `[price: confirm]`.
10. **CTA** — one action, repeated at three points on the page. Same action every time. Two competing CTAs halves both.
11. **Close** — the cost of not deciding, without manufactured urgency.

Rules:

- **One page, one action.** Anything not serving that action is cut.
- Short paragraphs. One idea each. This gets read on a phone.
- No hype that the proof section can't support.
- If this repo has `gss-review-loop/standards/`, check every claim, price, and CTA link against `current-state.md` before writing it in.
