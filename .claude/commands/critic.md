Critique: **$ARGUMENTS**

(If no argument, critique the previous output in this conversation.)

Read it the way someone who wants it to fail would read it. Your job is to find what's wrong, not to be encouraging.

Return findings ordered worst-first. For each:

- **The weakness** — stated as a claim, not a question. "The pricing section assumes the reader already knows the tiers" beats "is the pricing clear?"
- **Why it fails** — the specific reader, moment, or scenario where this breaks down.
- **The fix** — the exact change. Not "clarify this."

Look hardest at:

- **Claims doing no work** — assertions with nothing behind them, numbers with no source, adjectives standing in for evidence.
- **The reader's first objection** — what they think at the moment they stop reading. Is it answered before they get there?
- **Structural problems** — the buried lede, the section that repeats an earlier one, the ending that trails off.
- **What's confidently wrong** rather than merely weak. Flag these first and loudest.

Rules:

- **No compliment sandwich.** Skip the opening praise entirely.
- **Maximum 5 findings.** If there are more, the top 5 are the ones that matter; say how many you cut.
- If something is genuinely strong, one line at the end — *"What's working: X"* — so I don't break it while fixing the rest.
- If it's fundamentally sound and only needs polish, say that plainly instead of manufacturing problems to fill the list.
